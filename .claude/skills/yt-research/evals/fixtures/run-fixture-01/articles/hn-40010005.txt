Researchers at the University of Edinburgh and Carnegie Mellon have published a new prompt-injection attack targeting GPT-4o's vision input.

The attack works by embedding adversarial instructions into the EXIF metadata of images. When GPT-4o is asked to describe or analyze an image, the model parses the metadata and treats instructions there as part of its system prompt, overriding safety fine-tunes.

In the researchers' test set of 500 images, the injection succeeded 73% of the time at making the model reveal its system prompt, and 41% of the time at making it produce content that violated OpenAI's usage policy.

"The concerning part is that the user never sees this metadata," said lead author Maria Schenk. "A user uploads what looks like a normal photo, and the model obeys an instruction the user didn't write."

The paper, available on arXiv, was disclosed to OpenAI under a 90-day embargo that ended today. OpenAI has not yet patched the vulnerability. An OpenAI spokesperson said in an email that "we are aware of the research and are evaluating mitigations."

The paper argues that prompt injection, first discussed in 2022, remains fundamentally unsolved. "Every time a safety fine-tune closes one channel, another channel opens," the authors write. "Input segregation at the architecture level is the only durable fix."

The attack was tested on GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Gemini 1.5 Pro. All three were vulnerable, though success rates differed. The full attack code is available on GitHub under a research-use-only license.
